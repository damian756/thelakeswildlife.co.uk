Natural England, Climate Change Adaptation Manual, species account "Whitefish Coregonus lavaretus, Vendace Coregonus albula, and Arctic charr Salvelinus alpinus" (pp. 473-479). PDF fetched 11 Nov 2026; key passages transcribed from the PDF (not machine extracted).

p.474: "The vendace Coregonus albula has an extremely limited distribution in the UK and is considered the UK's rarest fish."
p.475: "In England, vendace is found in mesotrophic Bassenthwaite Lake and oligotrophic Derwent Water." "While Derwent Water has consistently supported vendace, none were found in Bassenthwaite Lake from 2001-2013 despite specific searches. Since 2013 a limited number of individuals have been found in Bassenthwaite Lake, suggesting the range may not have declined as previously suspected, and may be recovering, although it is premature to be confident about this."
p.475: Refuge attempts at Sprinkling Tarn: "There have been no reports of success from the site". "progeny from fish collected from Bassenthwaite Lake have successfully been established in Loch Skeen, Scotland."
p.475: Whitefish (schelly) in the Lake District at "Brothers Water, Haweswater, Red Tarn, and Ullswater"; refuge populations attempted at Small Water and Blea Water.
p.475: Arctic charr "in England and Wales is only found in large, deep, cold lakes." (No list of Lake District charr lakes in this account.)
p.476: photo caption "Arctic charr Salvelinus alpinus, male ... Ennerdale Water, Cumbria".
p.473 summary: nutrient enrichment and sediment are threats to these species.
